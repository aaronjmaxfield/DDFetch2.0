} Instructions:
  1. You must already be signed into DataDog in your browser for this to work.
  2. ServProvCode, Host, Environment and Begin/End Timestamp are required. Everything else is optional.
  3. Leave Scope on -- none -- and nothing is filtered out. You get everything logged for that agency and environment, which is deliberate: you can then narrow down inside DataDog and be certain nothing was removed before you looked.
  4. Pick a Scope once you know what you are investigating. That is what removes the routine noise. On a busy agency it is the difference between three million lines and twenty thousand.
  5. If a search comes back empty, that is a result worth doubting. Widen it before you conclude nothing happened -- clear the Scope, or check you have the right environment.
  6. Anything older than 15 days redirects to the rehydration page. When rehydrating, do not add extra search terms -- rehydrate everything, then filter in DataDog.
Tips:
  1. Civic Platform (biz) is selected for you by default, and it is usually worth leaving on. Most errors ACA displays come from the biz server, so an ACA search without it will often miss the line you are looking for.
  2. You can also untick everything and let a scope stand on its own, if the scope has logs of its own -- Payment > Forte, Paypal Commerce or SecurePay, and Documents > ADS or ACDS. That gives you just the adapter or document service, with no platform noise at all. The other scopes narrow a search rather than being a log source, so they still need an application ticked.
  3. For anything tied to a record, use the full CAP ID rather than the record number the customer quotes. Payments, documents and scripts all log the CAP ID. Reports and ADS are the exceptions -- they do log the record number.
  4. If searching for something in relation to a specific user, search by the username or additional parameter @USER_ID:<username-here> if for backoffice. For an ACA user, search their email or PUBLICUSER ID.
  5. Never assume a search for one value brings up everything related to it. Plenty of relevant lines do not contain the value you searched for -- find one line, then search its @TRACE_ID to get the whole request.
  6. Severity is not reliable. Script failures, IIS page failures and report problems are all logged as info or warn rather than error, so filtering by error status can hide the exact line you need.
